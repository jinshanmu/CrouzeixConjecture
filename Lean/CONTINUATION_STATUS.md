## Active v3 proof route

1. The finite complex Euclidean model, numerical range, functional calculus,
   positivity, spectrum, generated algebra, compactness, spectral inclusion,
   and Toeplitz--Hausdorff convexity are proved.
2. For the positive-real completion theorem, the auxiliary matrix `B` has a
   `SimpleDiagonalization`, while the target has the form
   `T = S diag(lambda) S⁻¹` in the same basis. Only
   `‖lambda i‖ ≤ 1` is assumed; repetitions and zeros are allowed.
3. The defect condition is relative to `alg(Bᴴ)`. Pullback produces the model
   `G(I-zΛ)⁻¹ + D(z)G`. The kernel is sampled at one point for each
   `lambda i` together with zero; the arbitrary-finite-type sampling theorem
   does not require these points to be distinct.
4. The unknown diagonal correction cancels. The resulting inequality passes
   through the two Gramians, the eigenvector argument, the first nonconstant
   Gramian term, square roots,
   and polar decomposition to prove `‖T‖ ≤ 2`. In manuscript notation the
   three matrices are `P,Q,Y`; Lean calls them
   `completionP, completionR, completionX`.
5. The double-layer construction produces a positive-real completion for the
   auxiliary pair `(B, f(B))`. Its pointwise companion values lie in `alg(B)`,
   so the defect lies in `alg(Bᴴ)`; no equality with the algebra generated by
   `f(B)` is needed.
6. Polynomial functional calculus preserves the eigenbasis:
   `SimpleDiagonalization.polynomialEval_eq_innerConjugation_diagonal`.
   Thus the target entries are `lambda i = f(βᵢ)`, with no injectivity
   requirement.
7. The fixed-simple theorem
   `norm_polynomialEval_le_two_mul_of_simpleSpectrum` treats `M = 0`
   directly. For `M > 0`, it uses only `f = p / M`, invokes the
   double-layer provider and auxiliary completion theorem, and rescales.
8. The remaining limits follow the v3 order: fix a canonical convex outer
   domain, let simple-spectrum `Bₖ → A` inside that domain, and only then
   shrink the domains to `W(A)`. These prove `crouzeixConjecture_mainTheorem`.
9. The rational corollary follows separately by polynomial approximation of
   functions pole-free on the numerical range.
